02 / More than merchandise

What traveled along the way?

Most merchants traveled only part of the network. Goods and ideas passed between many different hands.

01 — GOODS

Silk, spice & precious things

Chinese silk, Central Asian horses, Indian cotton, spices, glassware and other goods moved through interconnected markets. Different regions supplied different treasures.

COMMERCE CONNECTED DISTANT MARKETS
02 — KNOWLEDGE

Ideas that crossed borders

Paper, artistic techniques, scientific knowledge and stories spread between societies. These exchanges involved translation, adaptation and new inventions.

KNOWLEDGE GREW THROUGH EXCHANGE
03 — BELIEFS

Cultures in conversation

Buddhist monks traveled from South and Central Asia toward China. Christianity, Islam and other traditions also spread, alongside languages and artistic styles.

PEOPLE CARRIED THEIR WORLDVIEWS

Connection had a cost, too.

Deserts, mountain passes, war and political change made journeys dangerous. Interconnected land and sea routes also helped diseases spread, including plague in the fourteenth century.

03 / A changing world

Many centuries. Countless journeys.

138 BCE

Zhang Qian travels west

The Han envoy’s journeys helped expand Chinese knowledge of Central Asia and encouraged diplomatic and commercial connections.

1ST CENTURY CE

Luxury goods reach the Mediterranean

Goods such as Chinese silk reached Roman consumers through multiple intermediaries and both overland and maritime connections.

7TH–10TH CENTURIES

Cosmopolitan trading cities flourish

During the Tang period, Chang’an welcomed merchants and travelers. Oasis cities supported exchange, and ports connected growing sea networks.

13TH–14TH CENTURIES

The Mongol empires reshape travel

Mongol rule connected large parts of Eurasia and, in some periods and places, supported long-distance trade. Conflict and disease remained serious risks.

15TH CENTURY & BEYOND

Trade networks keep changing

Maritime commerce became increasingly important. Overland exchange continued: the Silk Roads did not end everywhere at a single moment.
